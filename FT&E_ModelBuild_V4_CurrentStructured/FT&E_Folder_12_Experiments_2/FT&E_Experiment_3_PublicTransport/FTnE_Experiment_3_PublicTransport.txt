
FT&E Experiment 3 – Public Transport Collapse and Emergence (London)

Objective:
To test the FT&E framework against real-world transport data during a known systemic disruption (COVID-19), measuring signs of contradiction, time-based forgiveness, and emergence across different transport systems.

Dataset:
Transport for London (TfL) – Number of journeys per reporting period by transport mode (Bus, Underground, DLR, Tram, Overground).
Period: 2019–2022 (filtered from full dataset)

Observations:

1. Collapse Phase (Q1–Q2 2020):
  - All transport modes exhibit a sharp and simultaneous collapse in ridership.
  - Reflects a systemic contradiction: disruption of mobility vs. expectation of flow.
  - Confirms FT&E contradiction signature: sudden collapse when recursive load exceeds system thresholds.

2. Forgiveness Phase (Q3 2020–2021):
  - Bus journeys showed the earliest signs of forgiveness and recovery.
  - Underground and Overground followed with slower, staggered re-emergence.
  - System exhibits time-regulated forgiveness loops—each mode absorbing shock and rebounding on a delay curve.

3. Emergence Phase (2021–2022):
  - Journey levels stabilize below pre-2020 levels.
  - New attractor states observed (stabilized values), different per mode.
  - Emergence confirmed: system did not restore, it restructured at new stable points.

Conclusion:
The experiment supports the FT&E hypothesis:
- Contradiction was absorbed through a period of forgiveness (spaced over time).
- Different subsystems (modes) expressed different recursive cycles.
- System did not reset—it emerged into a new state.
This validates FT&E as a descriptive pattern for feedback loop behavior, adaptation, and recursive emergence in public infrastructure.
