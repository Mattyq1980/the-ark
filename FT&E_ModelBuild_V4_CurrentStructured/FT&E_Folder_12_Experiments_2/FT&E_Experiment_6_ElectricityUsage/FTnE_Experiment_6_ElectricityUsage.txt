
FT&E Experiment 6 – Electricity Usage: Recursive Cycles in AEP Energy Demand

Objective:
To evaluate FT&E dynamics in a regional power grid by analyzing hourly energy consumption data from American Electric Power (AEP). We focused on daily patterns across multiple years, with a zoom into the year 2014 to track contradiction, forgiveness, and emergence.

Dataset:
PJM Interconnection LLC – AEP Hourly Energy Usage Data
Time Period: 2004–2018 (focused analysis on 2014)
Granularity: Hourly data, resampled to daily averages

Observations:

1. Contradiction Phase (Winter & Summer 2014):
  - Major surge in January and February, likely tied to the Polar Vortex.
  - Smaller but sustained surge during summer, reflecting HVAC load.
  - These events represent contradiction spikes—real-world pressure exceeding normal equilibrium.

2. Forgiveness Phase (Spring 2014):
  - Sharp drop in energy usage after winter surge.
  - Curve is smooth and recursive—pacing matches system relaxation after tension.
  - Forgiveness is not immediate—it unfolds over time, confirming FT&E’s temporal dynamics.

3. Emergence Phase (Late 2014):
  - Post-summer, the system stabilizes into a smoother curve.
  - This flattening shows emergent attractor formation—a new equilibrium until the next recursion.
  - No catastrophic failure observed—suggests resilient self-regulation.

Conclusion:
The experiment strongly validates FT&E principles in infrastructure systems:
- Contradiction manifests as natural seasonal overload.
- Forgiveness is environmentally and behaviorally paced.
- Emergence arises in the form of rhythmic stabilization, not return to baseline.

Electricity usage is a perfect analog for recursive human–environment feedback systems. AEP’s 2014 profile offers a clean case of systemic contradiction and forgiveness loops giving rise to a temporary, emergent equilibrium.
