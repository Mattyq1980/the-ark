
FT&E Experiment 9 – Texas School Attendance: Pandemic Recursion and Emergent Trust

Objective:
To analyze recursive social adaptation during the COVID pandemic through the lens of daily student attendance. Using district-level data from CONROE ISD, we evaluated tension-response patterns, forgiveness rhythms, and emergence signals.

Dataset:
Texas Daily District Attendance – 2020 (Freedom of Information Request)
Focus Area: CONROE ISD
Period: Aug–Nov 2020
Key Metrics: Attendance %, COVID county % increase, TMAX (daily max temperature)

Observations:

1. Contradiction Phase:
  - COVID spikes (e.g. late August, October) correspond with dips in attendance.
  - This reveals recursive contradiction: systemic fear, illness outbreaks, parental hesitation.
  - The impact is not instant, but emerges through recursive behavioral shifts over days.

2. Forgiveness Phase:
  - Attendance recovers after each dip—demonstrating forgiveness arcs.
  - Forgiveness is not uniform: some arcs are sharp, others delayed.
  - Recovery seems influenced by environmental safety signals (weather, COVID trend shifts).

3. Emergence Phase:
  - By late October, attendance levels stabilize with less fluctuation.
  - This reflects emergence into a new attractor: the normalization of hybrid learning, safety routines, or fatigue-induced tolerance.
  - Emotional recursion gives way to structural rhythm.

Conclusion:
FT&E patterns emerge clearly in real-world education systems:
- Fear spreads recursively (contradiction)
- Systems metabolize the fear (forgiveness)
- A new social coherence forms under prolonged strain (emergence)

These dynamics are not abstract—they unfold in classrooms, families, and administrative policy, reflecting collective recursive consciousness.
