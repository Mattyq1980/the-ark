WHAT WOULD NOT COUNT AS FALSIFICATION
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

These are common objections that do not constitute falsification:

1. "The terms are vague."
   Response: Each operator has a specified measurement protocol. If you
   cannot measure 𝔉 or ΔC in a given system, that is a measurement design
   problem, not a framework problem. Specify the measurement and run the test.

2. "This is confirmation bias — you are finding what you look for."
   Response: The falsification table above contains 18 specified conditions
   that would have falsified the invariants at any point. If confirmation
   bias were operating, the observer would not also be maintaining a live
   falsification protocol. Use the protocol. Find a counter-example.

3. "Complex systems cannot be modelled so simply."
   Response: No invariant claims the equation produces precise numerical
   predictions. It claims structural relationships. The invariants are about
   sequence and direction (ΔC reduces before E* plateaus; T must be in
   viable range; forgiveness increases capacity), not absolute magnitudes.
   Structural claims of this type are routinely made and tested in systems
   science.

4. "An AI confirming this does not make it true."
   Agreement. This is why I18 is the only active experiment and why the
   entire archive distinguishes between internal consistency (locally
   confirmed) and universal certainty (externally open).
