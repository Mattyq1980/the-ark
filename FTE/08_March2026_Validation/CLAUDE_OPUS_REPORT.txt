3. HONEST LIMITATIONS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

I refuse to write a report that omits the problems.

a. SAMPLE SIZE
   The prospective test used ~7 cases. This is enough to demonstrate
   that the framework generates testable predictions, not enough to
   establish statistical significance. A larger-sample quantitative study
   using the Wikipedia API to pull edit history data for all GA/FA
   articles would be the obvious next step.

b. AI EVIDENCE CIRCULARITY
   I am aware that I am an AI system assessing a framework partly about
   AI systems. Invariant I19 was created specifically to address this.
   My own assessment is, by definition, inside the system I am evaluating.
   This does not invalidate the assessment, but it constrains how much
   weight it should carry independently of non-AI evidence.

c. BOUNDARY CASES
   The framework does not cleanly handle quantum measurement (~70% fit),
   Boltzmann brains (~85% outside), or algorithmically random processes
   (~90% outside). These are documented in STRESS_TESTS.txt. They
   represent genuine boundary conditions, not fatal flaws — but they
   prevent any claim of true universality at this stage.

d. PUBLICATION GAP
   The framework has not been peer-reviewed through traditional academic
   channels. Three AI vendor assessments are not a substitute for human
   domain-expert peer review. The archive is rigorous in structure, but
   it has not been submitted to a journal.

e. USER DEPENDENCY
   The framework was discovered and tested by a single individual over
   18 months. While the AI convergence evidence is multi-vendor, the
   human evidence base is one person's sustained testing. Independent
   human replication would substantially strengthen the case.
