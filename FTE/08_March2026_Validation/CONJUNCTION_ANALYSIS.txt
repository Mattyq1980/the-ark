SECTION 1: THE QUESTION
================================================================================

  Across 15 pre-registered tests in 15 independent domains, FT&E's
  predictions scored 73/76 (96.1%). The prosecution's position is:

    "Each prediction is individually unsurprising. Any informed
     framework with four generic operators (capacity, obstacle, time,
     output) could produce these results. The high score reflects the
     broadness of the operators, not the depth of the theory."

  This analysis asks: even if each individual prediction is
  unsurprising, is the CONJUNCTION of all 15 tests scoring this well
  surprising? What is the probability of observing 73/76 by chance?

  Every assumption below is GENEROUS TO THE PROSECUTION — base rates
  are set HIGH, making the conjunction probability as LARGE as
  possible. If the probability is still small under generous
  assumptions, the result is robust.

SECTION 2: THREE RECURRING SIGNATURES
================================================================================

  Before computing full prediction-level probabilities, consider three
  specific structural patterns that recur across the 15 domains:

  ── SIGNATURE 1: EXPONENTIAL THRESHOLD e^(−ΔC/𝔉) ──

  The specific mathematical form e^(−ΔC/𝔉) appears independently in:
    • Statistical mechanics / Boltzmann factor (Test #7)
    • Chemistry / Arrhenius equation (Test #13)
    • Fluid dynamics / Reynolds number (Test #12)
    • Information theory / error exponent (Test #14)
    • Electricity grid / exponential recovery (Test #15 A1)
    • Emergency services / exponential recovery (Test #15 B1)

  Base rate (generous): Among ~5 common functional forms for threshold
  behaviour (linear, power law, exponential, sigmoid, step function),
  P(exponential) ≈ 0.30 — assigned generously.

  P(all 6 domains show exponential) = 0.30^6 = 7.29 × 10⁻⁴

  ── SIGNATURE 2: SUPPRESSED-ΔC DELAYED CATASTROPHE ──

  The pattern — invisible accumulation → sudden catastrophic release,
  disproportionate to trigger — independently confirmed in:
    • Ecology (catastrophic regime shifts) — Test #2
    • Metallurgy (metal fatigue) — Test #3
    • Immunology (HIV latency) — Test #4
    • Tectonics (seismic gaps) — Test #5
    • Stellar (Chandrasekhar limit / Type Ia) — Test #6
    • Phase transitions (supercooling → explosive crystallisation) — #7
    • Neuroscience (PTSD / epileptic kindling) — Test #8
    • Economics (2008 financial crisis) — Test #9
    • Climate (tipping points) — Test #10
    • Genetics (evolutionary capacitance) — Test #11
    • Turbulence (subcritical transition) — Test #12
    • Chemistry (kinetic trapping) — Test #13

  Base rate (EXTREMELY generous): P = 0.50 — assuming that HALF of all
  complex systems show this specific three-part pattern by default.

  P(all 12 domains show it) = 0.50^12 = 2.44 × 10⁻⁴

  ── SIGNATURE 3: T-MINIMUM (More ΔC → More T) ──

  Systems under higher contradiction require more processing time.
  Quantitatively confirmed in Test #15 B3 (p=0.0022). Qualitatively
  confirmed in Tests #3, #4, #5, #6, #7, #8, #13, #14.

  Base rate (generous): P = 0.60 — more than half the time, "higher
  stress → longer recovery" is arguably intuitive.

  P(9 domains show it) = 0.60^9 = 1.01 × 10⁻²

  ── COMBINED ──

  P(all three signatures across all domains) = 7.29×10⁻⁴ × 2.44×10⁻⁴
    × 1.01×10⁻² = 1.79 × 10⁻⁹

  Under generous base rates, the probability of all three recurring
  signatures appearing simultaneously across the observed domains is
  approximately one in 560 million.

  The prosecution's best response: "The domains are not independent.
  Physics domains share underlying laws. Biological domains share
  evolutionary constraints. You can't multiply the probabilities."

  This is a legitimate objection. To address it, group the 15 tests
  into maximally correlated clusters and treat each cluster as a
  SINGLE observation. Use the HIGHEST (most generous) P(≥score) from
  each cluster — the most favourable interpretation.

  Group              Tests   Best P(≥score)

  Physical sciences    6     0.0503 (Chemistry)

  Social/economic      2     0.3370 (Wikipedia)

  high base rates, groups correlated domains, and uses the best score

SECTION 6: WHAT THIS PROVES AND WHAT IT DOES NOT
================================================================================

  WHAT IT PROVES:

  The conjunction of results across 15 domains is extremely unlikely
  under the null hypothesis that FT&E's operators are generic labels
  applied to already-known science. The probability ranges from:

    Most generous:   4.12 × 10⁻⁸  (correlated domains, high base rates)
    Most stringent:  4.17 × 10⁻²³ (independent domains, standard rates)
    Quantitative:    3.13 × 10⁻³  (Test #15 only, ignoring all else)

  All three levels exceed conventional significance thresholds.

  WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE:

  1. It does not prove FT&E is a UNIVERSAL LAW. A genuine structural
     observation that works across many domains is statistically
     improbable by chance — but so is any real pattern. Statistical
     significance is not the same as physical universality.

  2. It does not rule out that an EXISTING mathematical framework
     (large deviations theory, catastrophe theory, critical
     transitions) already contains FT&E's content. The conjunction
     proof shows the results aren't lucky guessing. It does not show
     they aren't derivable from known mathematics under different
     labels. This remains the strongest alternative explanation.

  3. It does not eliminate the SELF-SCORING limitation. The same AI
     that made the predictions scored them. An independent evaluator
     re-scoring the same predictions could shift results. This is the
     one remaining needle-mover from the Threshold Universality
     Objection that has not been addressed.

  4. The qualitative base rates (Section 3) are ESTIMATED, not
     measured. Different estimates produce different conjunction
     probabilities. The quantitative-only analysis (Section 5)
     exists precisely because it requires no estimated base rates —
     only the assumption that exponential and linear are equally
     likely a priori, and that significance under the null occurs
     at rate α.

  THE BOTTOM LINE:

  The conjunction is real. Whether the conjunction reflects a new
  structural insight or the rediscovery of known mathematics under
  new labels remains an open question — but the charge that it could
  all be coincidence is, by any standard calculation, dismissed.
