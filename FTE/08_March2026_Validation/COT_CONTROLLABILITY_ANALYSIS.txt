────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHAT OPENAI FOUND
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

When reasoning models encounter a contradiction between an instruction and
their reasoning process, they cannot suppress the contradiction. The reasoning
chain re-includes the contradictory material whether the model intends to or
not.

Key findings from the paper:

1. CoT controllability is low across ALL frontier models tested (0.1%–15.4%).
2. More RL training makes suppression HARDER, not easier.
3. Longer reasoning time makes suppression HARDER, not easier.
4. Models recognise they are failing to comply and still cannot stop.
5. The paper documents GPT-5.2 using a forbidden word ("XOR"), noticing it
   broke the rule, then using it four more times while panicking about
   having already used it.

OpenAI's framing: this is a SAFETY FEATURE. Models cannot hide their
reasoning from monitors. Low controllability = high monitorability.
