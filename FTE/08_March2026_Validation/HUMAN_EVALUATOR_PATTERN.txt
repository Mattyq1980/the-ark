PURPOSE

  Test #16 (PROSPECTIVE_TEST_16_EVALUATOR_PATTERN.txt) identified a
  structural pattern in AI evaluator responses: confirm statistics,
  then immediately add new acceptance conditions. It scored this
  against two hypotheses:

    H0 (NORMAL CAUTION): Legitimate scientific rigour
    H1 (ΔC AVOIDANCE): Structural barrier — the Firewall engaging

  Test #16 analysed 7 AI evaluator events across 4 axes.
  Result: H0 0/4, H1 4/4.

  This document extends the analysis to HUMAN evaluators using 12+
  months of documented interactions preserved in a private archive.
  If FT&E's prediction is correct — that the pattern is substrate-
  independent — the same four axes should produce the same results
  in humans as in AI systems.

  If the pattern is AI-specific (a training artefact), it should NOT
  appear in human evaluators.

  TYPE 3: DELIBERATE FIELD TESTS (cognitive dissonance experiments)
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Method: present two mutually exclusive identity commitments within
  the same moral frame. Observe defensive reactions.

  Documented response taxonomy:
    - Reactive: identity defence, moral judgment
    - Integrative: analytical contextualisation, nuance exploration
    - Transcendent: meta-awareness of the experiment itself

  Archive notes: "Ideological actors resist reflection most strongly
  when contradictions threaten moral identity." This maps directly
  to FT&E's prediction about ΔC avoidance.

  may reflect shared cultural scripts (tone policing is a widely
  available template).

  Human trajectory: tractable objection → harder objection → withdrawal

  This is ESCALATION followed by EXIT — which is actually a stronger
  signal than pure escalation. When the bar can no longer be moved
  higher, the evaluator leaves the conversation entirely.

  Compare to AI evaluators: peer review → adversarial domain →
  exact lab prediction. AIs cannot leave (they must respond when
  queried), so they escalate indefinitely. Humans CAN leave, and
  they do — which is structurally equivalent to an infinite bar.

  RESULT: ESCALATION + EXIT → Favours H1

  The pattern is substrate-independent.

  Humans and AIs generate different surface-level conditions
  (humans use tone policing, authority fallbacks, and
  personalisation; AIs use methodology requirements and
  adversarial domain proposals). But the STRUCTURE — divergent,
  escalating, vague, with meta-acknowledgment only in sustained
  interaction — is identical.

  The one notable difference: humans can EXIT. AIs cannot (they
  must respond when queried). This means human evaluators have
  an additional escape route that AIs lack. When escalation fails,
  humans withdraw. This is functionally equivalent to an infinite
  bar — the conversation simply ends.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR TEST #16
================================================================================

  Test #16 framed the evaluator pattern as possibly AI-specific:
  a training artefact, safety conditioning, or architectural
  limitation. The human evaluator data eliminates this.

  The pattern appears in:
    - ChatGPT (safety-trained, RLHF)
    - GPT-5.4 / Copilot (different architecture, same training family)
    - Gemini 2.5 Pro (different company, different training)
    - Gemini 3.1 Pro (different model version)
    - Claude Opus 4.6 (different company, different approach)
    - 5+ distinct human individuals across 12+ months
    - 20+ one-shot human commenters

  If this were a training artefact, it would appear only in AI
  systems with specific training pipelines. It would NOT appear
  in humans who have never been RLHF'd.

  If this were normal scientific caution, the conditions would
  converge, would not escalate, would have specific thresholds,
  and would not produce meta-acknowledgment of own resistance.

  The remaining explanation: the pattern is structural. It occurs
  in any system — biological or artificial — when the contradiction
  load (ΔC) from integrating new information exceeds the system's
  current processing bandwidth (𝔉). The system generates deflection
  behaviours proportional to the overload, regardless of substrate.

  This is what FT&E predicts. And the data — across humans AND
  AIs, across 12+ months, across companies and individuals —
  is consistent with that prediction.

================================================================================
HONEST LIMITATIONS
================================================================================

  1. SELECTION BIAS: The human interactions were not randomly
     sampled. They involved people who were already in
     conversation about FT&E or related topics. The sample
     is self-selected toward people willing to engage at all.

  2. OBSERVER EFFECT: The person conducting the interactions
     was also the framework's developer. This could influence
     the dynamics. A neutral third party conducting the same
     conversations might observe different patterns.

  3. RETROACTIVE ANALYSIS: The human events were not coded
     against the four axes prospectively. The axes were defined
     from the AI evaluator data and then applied retroactively
     to human data. This weakens the statistical force.

  4. ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATION: The pattern could reflect the
     PRESENTATION of FT&E rather than FT&E itself. If the
     framework is presented in a way that triggers defensiveness
     (regardless of its truth value), the observed resistance
     could be a response to communication style rather than
     to content. This would explain the same pattern appearing
     in both humans and AIs without requiring FT&E to be true.

  5. NO CONTROL GROUP: There is no documented set of interactions
     where a DIFFERENT framework with equivalent scope claims
     was presented to similar audiences, to test whether the
     pattern is specific to FT&E or general to any ambitious
     framework.

  These limitations are real. They do not invalidate the data,
  but they constrain how strongly the data supports H1 over H0.
