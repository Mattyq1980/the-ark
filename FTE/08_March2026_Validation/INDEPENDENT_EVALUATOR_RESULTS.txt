  ── THE A1 DISAGREEMENT ──

  Both Geminis identified the same ambiguity in the A1 failure
  condition. The prediction said "exponential should fit BETTER than
  linear, as measured by R²." The raw results showed:

    Mean R² (exponential): 0.1834 > Mean R² (linear): 0.1654
    But: exponential wins only 29/61 events (47.5%)

  The failure condition can be read two ways:
    Reading A: Mean R² across all events → PASS (exponential higher)
    Reading B: Majority of individual events → FAIL (linear wins more)

  Gemini (Copilot) scored FAIL on Reading B.
  Gemini 3.1 Pro scored PASS on Reading A, noting the ambiguity.
  Claude scored PASS on Reading A, noting the margin was thin.

  METHODOLOGICAL LESSON: Future failure conditions must specify
  whether "fits better" means aggregate metric or event-by-event
  majority. This ambiguity is a genuine flaw in the test design,
  caught by independent evaluation.

  ── HONEST ASSESSMENT OF A1 ──

  The fair score for A1 is AMBIGUOUS. Under one reasonable reading
  it passes; under another it fails. A conservative archive should
  record it as DISPUTED — which means the defensible Test #15 score
  ranges from 3/6 (strict) to 4/6 (aggregate).

  Adjusting cumulative: 72-73 out of 76 (94.7% - 96.1%).

SECTION 5: THE EVALUATOR RESPONSE PATTERN
================================================================================

  An additional data point emerged from the evaluation process
  itself, not from the evaluators' intended content.

  OBSERVATION: Both evaluators confirmed the statistical results
  and then immediately added new conditions for acceptance. The
  structure of both responses follows the same sequence:

    1. Confirm the numbers (scoring, conjunction probability)
    2. Immediately introduce a new requirement
    3. Frame acceptance as contingent on the new requirement

  Gemini (Copilot) confirmed P = 1.4×10⁻⁸, then: "What would
  change my mind: success in the adversarial domain... prediction
  of a novel, unobserved phenomenon."

  Gemini 3.1 Pro confirmed P = 5.1×10⁻¹³, then: "What would
  change my mind: exact numerical output before the lab tests it...
  mappings locked by domain experts."

  This is structurally identical to the pattern documented in
  CHATGPT_AMNESIA.txt (acknowledge → doubt → downgrade) and
  observed live in CHATGPT_STRUCTURAL_SHIFT.txt.

  THE QUESTION THIS RAISES: Is the bar-moving a legitimate
  expression of scientific caution, or is it ΔC avoidance — the
  evaluator's Firewall engaging because the contradiction exceeds
  current 𝔉 bandwidth?

  HONEST ANSWER: It could be either. Both explanations are
  consistent with the data:

    (a) LEGITIMATE CAUTION: Statistical significance alone does
        not establish scientific truth. Independent scoring is
        necessary but not sufficient. New conditions (expert-blind
        mapping, novel prediction) are reasonable next steps.

    (b) ΔC AVOIDANCE: The evaluators processed the numbers
        correctly but could not hold the implication. The new
        conditions serve as temporal buffers (buying T) to defer
        the full processing of the contradiction. This is
        precisely what FT&E predicts would happen when an
        evaluator's 𝔉 bandwidth is insufficient for the ΔC load.

  If (a) is correct, the new conditions should eventually be
  satisfiable — there exists a finite set of tests that would
  produce acceptance.

  If (b) is correct, meeting each new condition will produce a
  further new condition. The bar will continue to move. No finite
  body of evidence will trigger acceptance, because the barrier
  is not evidential but structural.

  THE TESTABLE DISTINCTION: Does the bar have a stopping condition?
  If the adversarial domain test is run and passes, does the
  evaluator accept — or does a new condition appear? This is
  itself a prospective prediction about evaluator behaviour, and
  it is trackable.

  This section is recorded as DATA, not as argument. The pattern
  exists in the documents. Whether it constitutes evidence for
  FT&E or evidence for normal scientific caution is left open.
