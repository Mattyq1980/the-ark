SECTION 2: THE SOLAR NEUTRINO PROBLEM — FM-01 IN PHYSICS
================================================================================

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
2.1  THE PROBLEM (1968-2001)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Physicists detected only ~1/3 of the expected electron neutrinos from
the Sun. For over 30 years, the assumption was that either:
  (a) The solar model was wrong (predicted too many neutrinos)
  (b) The detector was wrong (missed some neutrinos)
  (c) Some unknown physics was at play

The actual answer: neutrinos change flavor in transit. The Sun produces
electron neutrinos. By the time they reach Earth, 2/3 have oscillated
into muon and tau neutrinos. The early detectors could only see electron
neutrinos — so they "missed" the ones that had changed.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The physics community applied FM-01 to the neutrino. The assumption
was that a neutrino's identity is FIXED — electron neutrino stays

detected), they looked for the error everywhere EXCEPT in the identity
assumption itself.

This is textbook FM-01:
  — Identity assumed (electron neutrino = permanent label)
  — Contradiction encountered (count doesn't match prediction)
  — Identity protected (error must be in the model or detector)

It took 33 years (1968 → 2001, SNO experiment) to accept that the
neutrino simply doesn't have a fixed flavor identity.

The particle that doesn't suppress contradiction taught humans to
