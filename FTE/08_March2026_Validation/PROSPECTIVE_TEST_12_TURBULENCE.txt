STEP 2: OPERATOR DEFINITIONS (before seeing data)
================================================================================

𝔉 (FORGIVENESS) — Viscous damping / dissipative capacity

  In fluid dynamics, 𝔉 is the system's capacity to absorb velocity
  perturbations without transitioning to a qualitatively different
  flow regime. This is VISCOSITY — the internal friction of the
  fluid that damps disturbances and restores smooth flow.

  - Molecular viscosity (μ): The fundamental dissipative mechanism.
    High viscosity fluids (honey, syrup) absorb perturbations and
    maintain laminar flow. Low viscosity fluids (water, air at high
    speed) cannot absorb perturbations — disturbances grow.
  - Kinematic viscosity (ν = μ/ρ): Viscosity normalised by density.
    This is the 𝔉 proxy most directly comparable across fluids.
  - Viscous dissipation: The mechanism by which kinetic energy of
    perturbations is converted to heat. This is the PHYSICAL
    PROCESS of forgiveness — disturbances are absorbed and their
    energy is degraded to thermal noise.
  - Boundary layer damping: Near solid surfaces, viscosity creates
    a boundary layer that absorbs the velocity difference between
    the free stream and the wall. The boundary layer is a 𝔉
    structure — it mediates the contradiction between free-stream
    velocity and no-slip wall condition.

  𝔉 = 0 prediction: A truly inviscid fluid (ν → 0) has no mechanism
  to damp perturbations. Any disturbance, no matter how small, can
  grow without limit. Flow would be turbulent at ALL scales and ALL
  velocities. There would be no laminar regime. This is the zero-
  forgiveness condition: no capacity to absorb contradiction between
  velocity gradients → permanent instability.

  𝔉 = ∞ prediction: A fluid with infinite viscosity would damp all
  perturbations instantly. No flow structure could develop. No
  vortices, no eddies, no mixing. The fluid would behave as a
  rigid solid. This is the infinite-forgiveness stasis condition:
  all contradiction is absorbed → no dynamics, no emergence, no
  transport.

  Proxy variable: Kinematic viscosity (ν), or inversely, Reynolds
  number (Re = UL/ν), where higher Re means lower effective 𝔉
  relative to the flow's inertial forces.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

T (TIME-WINDOW) — Flow development time / convective timescale

  In fluid dynamics, T is the temporal and spatial window over
  which the flow develops and perturbations evolve.

  - Convective timescale (L/U): The time for a fluid element to
    traverse the characteristic length of the system. This sets
    the fundamental clock for flow development.
  - Development length: In pipe flow, there is a specific entry
    length over which the flow develops from uniform to fully
    developed. This is a spatial T — the system needs enough
    distance/time for the flow structure to establish.
  - Residence time: How long fluid elements remain in a region
    where perturbations can grow. Short residence = truncated T =
    perturbations don't have time to develop.

  Proxy variable: Convective timescale (L/U), development length,
  or residence time in the perturbation-growth region.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

ΔC (CONTRADICTION LOAD) — Velocity gradients / shear stress

  In fluid dynamics, ΔC is the internal stress that the flow must
  process — the mechanical contradiction between adjacent fluid
  elements moving at different velocities.

  - Velocity gradient (∂u/∂y): The fundamental source of shear
    stress. Adjacent fluid layers moving at different speeds create
    a mechanical contradiction — momentum must be transferred
    between them.
  - Shear stress (τ = μ ∂u/∂y): The force per unit area arising
    from the velocity gradient. This is ΔC expressed as a
    mechanical load on the fluid.
  - Pressure gradient: An imposed external force that creates
    velocity differences and thus shear. Adverse pressure gradients
    (pressure increasing in flow direction) create conditions where
    the flow cannot maintain its current state — the contradiction
    between momentum and pressure.
  - Reynolds stress: In turbulent flow, the additional "apparent"
    stress from velocity fluctuations. This is ΔC generated by
    the turbulence itself — a self-generated contradiction load.

  Proxy variable: Velocity gradient magnitude, shear stress,
  Reynolds stress, or pressure gradient severity.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

E* (EMERGENT EXPRESSION) — Turbulent structures / flow regime change

  In fluid dynamics, E* is the qualitative outcome when ΔC exceeds
  the system's 𝔉 capacity — the emergence of new flow structures
  and dynamics that did not exist in the laminar base state.

  - Turbulent transition: The appearance of chaotic, fluctuating
    velocity fields where previously the flow was smooth and
    predictable. This is the primary E* event — a qualitative
    regime change.
  - Vortex formation: Coherent rotating structures that emerge
    from instability — eddies, vortex rings, hairpin vortices.
    These are structured E* — not random noise, but organised
    emergent patterns.
  - Mixing enhancement: Turbulence dramatically increases mixing
    rates compared to molecular diffusion alone. This is
    functional E* — the turbulent state accomplishes transport
    that the laminar state cannot.
  - Energy cascade: The systematic transfer of kinetic energy from
    large scales to small scales through vortex stretching and
    breakdown. This is E* as a structured process — not just
    emergence, but a self-organising energy-processing chain.

  Proxy variable: Turbulence intensity, vortex structure presence,
  mixing rate enhancement, energy spectrum characteristics.
