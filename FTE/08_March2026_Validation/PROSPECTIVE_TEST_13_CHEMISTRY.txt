  𝔉 (Forgiveness)  CATALYSIS / activation energy lowering.
                   The mechanism that reduces the energy barrier
                   to reaction — allows the system to process
                   reactant→product transformation that would
                   otherwise be kinetically blocked. Includes:
                   heterogeneous catalysis (surface), homogeneous
                   catalysis (same phase), enzyme catalysis
                   (biological). The catalyst is NOT consumed —
                   it facilitates processing without being
                   destroyed. Also: TEMPERATURE as thermal energy
                   providing kinetic energy to overcome barriers.

  T (Time)         REACTION TIMESCALE / residence time / contact
                   time. The duration over which reactants interact.
                   Includes: reaction half-life, mean free time
                   between molecular collisions, equilibration time.
                   Too short = reaction incomplete. Too long = side
                   reactions dominate. There should be an optimal
                   T window for desired product formation.

  ΔC (Contradiction) THERMODYNAMIC DRIVING FORCE / chemical potential
                   difference / concentration gradient / departure
                   from equilibrium. The "distance" between current
                   state and equilibrium. Reactant concentration
                   exceeding equilibrium position. Also: activation
                   energy itself as the BARRIER that must be overcome
                   — the contradiction between "reactants want to
                   become products" and "the energy barrier prevents
                   it."

  E* (Emergence)   REACTION PRODUCTS / new chemical species / novel
                   molecular structures. The substances that did not
                   exist before the reaction — genuinely new molecular
                   arrangements. Also: self-organisation in chemical
                   systems (oscillating reactions, pattern formation).

PREDICTION 1: THRESHOLD TRANSITION — ACTIVATION ENERGY AS ΔC/𝔉 GATE

  The reaction rate should depend on a THRESHOLD parameter: the ratio
  of the energy barrier (ΔC) to the available thermal energy (𝔉).
  There should exist a specific mathematical relationship — probably
  exponential — between the rate and this ratio. Below threshold:
  reaction is kinetically blocked (effectively zero rate). Above
  threshold: reaction proceeds. The transition should be SHARP
  (exponential sensitivity to the barrier/temperature ratio, not
  linear).

PREDICTION 2: CATALYSIS AS 𝔉 BANDWIDTH EXPANSION

  Catalysts should work by LOWERING THE ACTIVATION BARRIER — not by
  changing the thermodynamic outcome (products), but by providing an
  alternative pathway with lower energy requirement. Specific sub-
  predictions:
  (a) Catalysts do not change equilibrium position (they don't change
      ΔC or E*) — they change the RATE (the 𝔉 pathway).
  (b) Catalysts are not consumed — they facilitate processing without
      being destroyed (𝔉 that persists).
  (c) Removing the catalyst should BLOCK the reaction (or massively
      slow it) even if thermodynamics favours the products — kinetic
      trapping. This is 𝔉 = 0 → system stuck despite ΔC.
  (d) Catalyst poisoning should be a specific, studied phenomenon:
      something that disables 𝔉 and traps the system.

PREDICTION 3: LE CHATELIER AS ΔC RESPONSE — EQUILIBRIUM RESTORATION

  When a system at equilibrium is PERTURBED (ΔC injected), the system
  should respond by shifting to COUNTERACT the perturbation — moving
  to reduce the imposed ΔC. Specific sub-predictions:
  (a) This should be a UNIVERSAL principle applying to all equilibrium
      systems (concentration, pressure, temperature perturbations).
  (b) The response should be PROPORTIONAL to the perturbation
      magnitude — larger ΔC produces larger compensating shift.
  (c) The system should reach a NEW equilibrium, not return to the
      old one — the perturbation is metabolised into a new state (E*).

PREDICTION 4: T-WINDOW — KINETIC VS THERMODYNAMIC CONTROL

  There should be an optimal time/temperature window for desired
  products. Specifically:
  (a) T too short / temperature too low: reaction doesn't proceed,
      system stays trapped in reactant state (kinetic control —
      insufficient 𝔉·T).
  (b) T adequate / temperature optimal: desired products form
      selectively.
  (c) T too long / temperature too high: side reactions, decomposition,
      or thermodynamic sink products dominate — the system overshoots
      to the lowest-energy state, not the most useful one. This is
      T_too_long / 𝔉_too_high.
  (d) There should be a well-known distinction in chemistry between
      "kinetic products" and "thermodynamic products" — different
      outcomes depending on the T/temperature window.

PREDICTION 5: AUTOCATALYSIS AND CHEMICAL EMERGENCE

  There should exist chemical systems where the PRODUCT of the reaction
  CATALYSES its own formation — autocatalysis. This is E* feeding back
  into 𝔉. Specific sub-predictions:
  (a) Autocatalytic reactions should show SIGMOIDAL kinetics — slow
      start (low product = low catalysis), then exponential acceleration
      (product accumulates → more catalysis), then saturation (reactants
      depleted).
  (b) There should be oscillating chemical reactions — systems that
      cycle between states rather than reaching static equilibrium.
      This would be ΔC and 𝔉 cycling in a feedback loop.
  (c) These systems should be relevant to origin-of-life chemistry —
      the emergence of self-replicating chemical systems from non-
      living precursors. E* at the chemical level producing the
      conditions for biological E*.
