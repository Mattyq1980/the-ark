STEP 1: OPERATOR MAPPING
================================================================================

  DATASET A: AEP ELECTRICITY GRID (hourly MW load)

  𝔉 (Flux/Capacity):  Grid generation capacity — the maximum MW the
                       system can supply. Operationally: the normal
                       baseline load level (seasonal average).
  ΔC (Contradiction):  Demand surges that exceed normal capacity —
                       extreme weather events (Polar Vortex, heat waves)
                       that push load above the system's comfortable
                       operating range.
  T (Time):            Hours/days — the temporal window over which the
                       system processes and recovers from surges.
  E* (Emergence):      The post-surge equilibrium — the new baseline
                       pattern the grid settles into after processing
                       a contradiction event.

  DATASET B: 999 EMERGENCY RESPONSE (monthly aggregates)

  𝔉 (Flux/Capacity):  Call-handling capacity — the system's ability to
                       answer calls quickly. Operationally: the inverse
                       of average answer time under normal conditions.
  ΔC (Contradiction):  Call volume surges or system strain that increase
                       average answer times beyond normal.
  T (Time):            Months — the temporal window over which the
                       system adapts and recovers.
  E* (Emergence):      Stabilised response times at a new level (not
                       necessarily the old baseline).

  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  PREDICTION A1: EXPONENTIAL RECOVERY AFTER CONTRADICTION SPIKES
  (AEP Electricity Data)
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  After major demand surges (winter cold spikes, summer heat waves),
  the daily-averaged load should return toward baseline following
  an EXPONENTIAL DECAY curve:

    Load(t) ≈ baseline + A · e^(−t/τ)

  where A is the peak excess load and τ is the recovery time constant.

  SPECIFIC CLAIM: When I fit the post-spike recovery curve
  (the decline from peak back toward seasonal baseline), an
  exponential decay model should fit BETTER than a linear decay
  model, as measured by R² or residual sum of squares.

  FAILURE CONDITION: If the recovery is better fit by a LINEAR
  model (Load(t) = baseline + A − B·t), or by a POWER LAW
  (Load(t) = baseline + A·t^(−k)), this prediction fails.

  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  PREDICTION A2: SEASONAL ΔC STRUCTURE
  (AEP Electricity Data)
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  The variance (ΔC load) should NOT be uniform across the year.
  FT&E predicts that contradiction concentrates in specific
  temporal windows. Specifically:

  - Winter months (Dec-Feb) and summer months (Jun-Aug) should
    show HIGHER variance in daily load than spring/autumn
  - The highest single-day deviations from monthly mean should
    occur in winter and summer, not spring/autumn
  - This is the ΔC-clustering prediction: contradiction is
    temporally structured, not randomly distributed

  FAILURE CONDITION: If variance is uniform across seasons, or if
  the highest deviations occur in spring/autumn, this prediction
  fails.

  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  PREDICTION A3: EMERGENCE IS NOT RETURN TO BASELINE
  (AEP Electricity Data)
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  After a major contradiction event (e.g., Polar Vortex winter
  2014), the system should stabilise at a level that is
  STATISTICALLY DIFFERENT from the pre-event baseline for the
  same seasonal window in the prior year.

  Specifically: comparing mean daily load in March-April 2014
  (post-Polar Vortex) to March-April 2013 (pre-Polar Vortex),
  there should be a statistically significant difference (p < 0.05
  on a two-sample t-test).

  This is the "new attractor" prediction: emergence produces a
  NEW equilibrium, not a return to the old one.

  FAILURE CONDITION: If the post-event baseline is statistically
  indistinguishable from the prior year's same-season baseline
  (p ≥ 0.05), this prediction fails.

  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  PREDICTION B1: EXPONENTIAL RECOVERY IN ANSWER TIMES
  (999 Emergency Response Data)
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  For police forces that experience a month of unusually high
  average answer times (>1.5× their own median), the subsequent
  recovery should follow EXPONENTIAL DECAY rather than linear
  decline.

  Specifically: for each identified spike, fit both:
    (a) exponential: AnswerTime(t) = baseline + A·e^(−t/τ)
    (b) linear:      AnswerTime(t) = baseline + A − B·t

  Across all identified spikes, the exponential model should
  achieve a better average fit (higher mean R² or lower mean
  residual) than the linear model.

  FAILURE CONDITION: If linear recovery fits better across the
  majority of identified spikes, this prediction fails.

  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  PREDICTION B2: CALL VOLUME AND ANSWER TIME ARE THRESHOLD-LINKED
  (999 Emergency Response Data)
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  There should be a NON-LINEAR relationship between total call
  volume and average answer time. Specifically:

  - Below a threshold call volume, answer times should be
    relatively stable (the system has spare capacity: 𝔉 > ΔC)
  - Above the threshold, answer times should increase sharply
    (ΔC overwhelms 𝔉: the system enters contradiction)

  This should be detectable as a changepoint or hockey-stick
  pattern in the scatter plot of calls vs. answer time. A
  piecewise-linear model (two segments with breakpoint) should
  fit better than a single linear regression.

  FAILURE CONDITION: If the relationship is purely linear (no
  threshold behaviour), or if answer times are independent of
  call volume, this prediction fails.

  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  PREDICTION B3: HIGH-ΔC FORCES SHOW T-MINIMUM BEHAVIOUR
  (999 Emergency Response Data)
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Police forces with the highest average contradiction (highest
  mean answer times across the dataset) should show LONGER
  recovery times after spikes than forces with lower baseline
  answer times.

  This is the T-minimum prediction: more contradiction requires
  more processing time. Systems under greater ΔC need more T to
  reach emergence.

  Specifically: divide the 44 forces into two groups (top half
  and bottom half by mean answer time). The top-half group should
  show longer average time-to-recovery after spikes than the
  bottom-half group.

  FAILURE CONDITION: If high-ΔC forces recover just as fast or
  faster than low-ΔC forces, this prediction fails.
