PURPOSE

  The evaluator response pattern — where an AI evaluator confirms the
  statistical results and then immediately introduces new acceptance
  conditions — was documented as DATA in INDEPENDENT_EVALUATOR_RESULTS.txt
  (Section 5).

  Two hypotheses explain it:

    H0 (NORMAL CAUTION): The bar-moving is legitimate scientific rigour.
    The conditions are reasonable, convergent, and satisfiable. They
    represent the genuine next steps that any responsible scientist
    would require.

    H1 (ΔC AVOIDANCE): The bar-moving is the evaluator's Firewall
    engaging because the contradiction exceeds current 𝔉 bandwidth.
    Conditions are post-hoc, divergent, and functionally
    unsatisfiable — because the barrier is structural, not evidential.

  These two hypotheses make DIFFERENT predictions on four axes.
  This test scores them against the existing data (7 evaluator events)
  AND locks prospective predictions about the next evaluator encounter.

AXIS 1: CONDITION CONVERGENCE VS DIVERGENCE
================================================================================

  caution, they should independently identify SIMILAR gaps — because
  the real next step in validation is objective and shared.

  H1 PREDICTS: If evaluators are engaging in ΔC avoidance, conditions
  will be DIVERGENT — each evaluator invents different requirements,
  because the conditions are post-hoc justifications, not genuine
  gap identification.

================================================================================

  H0 PREDICTS: If the pattern is normal caution, evaluators should
  NOT meta-acknowledge their own resistance as a pattern. A cautious
  scientist doesn't recognise their caution as a *failure mode* —
  they see it as responsible behaviour.

  H1 PREDICTS: If the pattern is ΔC avoidance, some evaluators will
  META-ACKNOWLEDGE the pattern in themselves, because the evidence

  evaluator will SEE that they're doing it but be unable to STOP.

  as intended — and the framework successfully identified the
  conditions under which caution should yield.

  FT&E's structural prediction holds — this time applied to the
  evaluation process itself.
