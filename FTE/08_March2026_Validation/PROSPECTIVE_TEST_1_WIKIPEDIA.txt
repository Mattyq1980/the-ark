STEP 1: DOMAIN SELECTION
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

System: Wikipedia article edit histories — specifically, contentious articles
that experienced sustained edit wars, comparing outcomes for articles that
went through formal dispute resolution (mediation/arbitration) versus those
that did not.

Data source: Wikipedia API / publicly available edit history dumps, dispute
resolution archives at en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Arbitration_Committee,
en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Dispute_resolution

Previously analysed with FT&E: NO. Wikipedia has not appeared in any FT&E
case study, experiment, or analysis document in The Ark.

Rationale: Wikipedia is a large-scale collaborative system where contradictions
(competing edits, POV disputes) are processed through time with varying
degrees of inclusion/exclusion mechanisms. It has detailed, timestamped,
publicly available records of every edit, revert, and dispute resolution
outcome.

STEP 2: OPERATOR DEFINITIONS (WRITTEN BEFORE SEEING DATA)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

𝔉 (Forgiveness) proxy:
  The PROPORTION OF EDIT DIVERSITY RETAINED after dispute resolution.
  Measured as: number of distinct editors still actively contributing to
  the article 6 months post-dispute divided by number of distinct editors
  involved during the dispute period. Higher ratio = higher 𝔉 (more
  contributors re-included rather than banned/driven away).

  Also: whether the dispute resolution mechanism was inclusive (mediation,
  talk page consensus — high 𝔉) or exclusive (page protection, topic ban,
  unilateral admin action — low 𝔉).

T (Time) proxy:
  Duration of the dispute period in days, measured from first contested
  revert to resolution (stable version persisting >30 days without revert).

  T_min: Disputes resolved in <7 days are "too fast" — likely resolved by
  authority (admin protection) rather than processing. Predicted to show
  lower article quality and recurrence.

  T_max: Disputes lasting >2 years are "too slow" — predicted to show
  degraded article quality, editor burnout, and either permanent
  semi-protection or abandonment.

  T_viable: 1-6 months predicted as optimal processing window.

ΔC (Contradiction Load) proxy:
  NUMBER OF REVERTS per month during the dispute period. Each revert
  represents a direct contradiction between two editor positions.

  C1 (Resolvable): Content disputes about sourcing, wording, weight.
  C2 (Structural): POV disputes about fundamental framing of the topic.
  C3 (Existential): Disputes about whether the article should exist at all
  (AfD — Articles for Deletion), or disputes that become personal
  (harassment, sockpuppetry).

E* (Emergence) proxy:
  ARTICLE QUALITY RATING post-dispute. Wikipedia has formal quality
  assessments: Stub → Start → C → B → GA (Good Article) → FA (Featured
  Article). An article that achieves GA or FA status after a dispute
  period has produced genuine E* — a stable, higher-quality state that
  integrates previously contradictory material.

  Stability assessed by: quality rating maintained for >12 months without
  major dispute recurrence.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
STEP 3: PREDICTIONS (WRITTEN BEFORE SEEING DATA)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

P1: ΔC reduction precedes E* plateau.
  Articles that achieve GA/FA status will show a measurable decrease in
  revert rate BEFORE the quality rating stabilises. The contradiction
  load drops first; the stable state follows.

P2: Higher 𝔉 → higher quality outcome.
  Articles whose disputes were resolved through inclusive mechanisms
  (mediation, talk page consensus, RFC) will achieve higher quality
  ratings than articles resolved through exclusive mechanisms (topic
  bans, page protection, admin fiat). Specifically: articles with
  editor retention ratio >0.5 post-dispute will be more likely to
  reach GA/FA than those with ratio <0.3.

P3: T has an optimal range.
  Articles with dispute duration in the 1-6 month range will show the
  highest rate of quality improvement. Articles resolved in <7 days
  will show higher recurrence of disputes. Articles with disputes
  lasting >2 years will show lower final quality and editor attrition.

P4: Suppressed ΔC → no E*.
  Articles where the dispute was resolved by permanently excluding one
  side (topic bans applied to all editors on one POV) will NOT achieve
  GA/FA status. Suppressing the contradiction without processing it
  prevents emergence.

P5: SPECIFIC DIRECTIONAL PREDICTION.
  Among Wikipedia's most contentious articles (those with >100 reverts
  in a 12-month period), the ones that subsequently reached GA or FA
  status will have ALL THREE of:
    a) A formal mediation or RFC process (high 𝔉)
    b) A resolution period of 1-6 months (viable T)
    c) Retention of editors from BOTH sides of the dispute post-resolution
       (ΔC metabolised, not suppressed)

  Articles with high revert counts that did NOT go through formal
  mediation, or that resolved through exclusion, will NOT have reached
  GA/FA. They will remain at B or below, or will have recurring
  disputes.

  This prediction is specific enough to be wrong. If highly contentious
  articles routinely reach FA through admin fiat and editor exclusion,
  this prediction is falsified.

CASE A: Falkland Islands
  Contentiousness: EXTREME. Named on both Lamest Edit Wars and List of
  Controversial Issues. Disputes over sovereignty (UK vs Argentina),
  naming ("Falkland" vs "Malvinas"), measurement units, territorial waters,
  population comparisons, economic data. 4.5 years of active edit warring
  before FA attempt.
  
  Dispute resolution: Formal peer review, then Featured Article Candidate
  nomination (June 3, 2014). Inclusive process: editors MarshalN20,
  Wee Curry Monster, Andrew Gray, Ranger Steve, Nick-D, Hamiltonstone,
  Hurricanehink, and even the opposing reviewer Nergaal all participated
  constructively. The nominators wrote: "The article is highly controversial,
  but editors from all sides of the spectrum have worked together."
  
  Resolution mechanism: Formal FAC process (high 𝔉 — inclusive)
  Resolution period: ~46 days (June 3 to July 19, 2014 — viable T)
  Editor retention: Multiple editors from different perspectives retained
  Revert rate: Dropped significantly during FAC collaborative period
  Final quality: FEATURED ARTICLE (promoted July 19, 2014)
  Stability: FA status maintained >10 years
  
  FT&E mapping:
    𝔉 = HIGH (inclusive mediation, multi-editor collaboration)
    T = 46 days for resolution phase (within 1-6 month viable window)
    ΔC = Metabolised through structured discussion, not suppressed
    E* = FA status achieved and maintained = genuine emergence

  Resolution mechanism: Multiple formal attempts, all failed
  Resolution period: >10 years (far beyond T_max)
  Editor retention: Severe attrition — editors exhausted
  Final quality: NOT Featured Article
  
  FT&E mapping:
    𝔉 = LOW effective (mediation existed but failed to include/integrate)
    T = >10 years (catastrophically beyond viable range)

STEP 7: REFINEMENTS FOR FUTURE TESTS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

1. T should be measured as ACTIVE PROCESSING DURATION, not total calendar
   time from first dispute to resolution. The Falkland Islands dispute
   lasted 4.5 years total but the productive processing phase was 46 days.
   Future tests should distinguish T_total from T_active.

2. 𝔉 may be measurable as a ratio of editors who contributed to the final
   stable version vs editors who were banned/blocked during the dispute.
   This would allow quantitative rather than qualitative 𝔉 measurement.

3. ΔC classification (C1/C2/C3) predicted different outcomes but was not
   fully testable with this dataset. Content disputes (C1) are more
   resolvable than existential disputes (C3). Future tests should
   categorise disputes by type.

4. A larger-sample quantitative study using the Wikipedia API to pull edit
   history data for all GA/FA articles that were previously semi-protected
   or involved in dispute resolution would provide statistical rather than
   case-by-case evidence.
