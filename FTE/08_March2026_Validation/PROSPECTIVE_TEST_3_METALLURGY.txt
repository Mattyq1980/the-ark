STEP 2: OPERATOR DEFINITIONS (before seeing data)
================================================================================

𝔉 (FORGIVENESS) — Grain boundary mobility / atomic diffusion capacity

  In a metal under stress, atoms are displaced from equilibrium. Crystal
  defects (dislocations, vacancies, grain boundaries) represent frozen
  contradiction — the lattice "wants" to be in a lower energy state but
  cannot get there without the ability to rearrange.

  𝔉 in metallurgy = the system's capacity to allow atoms to move to new
  positions. This is primarily controlled by:
  - Temperature (thermal energy enables atomic diffusion)
  - Grain boundary mobility (boundaries between crystal grains can migrate)
  - Dislocation climb and glide (defects can move through the lattice)

  Higher 𝔉 = more atomic mobility = greater capacity to process
  internal stress. Lower 𝔉 = locked structure = contradiction frozen
  in place.

  Proxy variable: Temperature relative to melting point (homologous
  temperature), dislocation density reduction rate, grain boundary
  migration rate.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

T (TIME) — Processing duration at temperature

  Annealing requires sustained time at elevated temperature. The atoms
  need time to rearrange. This maps directly to FT&E's T operator.

  T_min = insufficient processing. The metal is heated but not long
  enough for atomic rearrangement to complete. Partial recovery only.
  Internal stresses remain.

  T_viable = sufficient time at correct temperature for full
  recrystallisation. New grain structure forms. Internal stress relieved.

  T_max = excessive time at temperature. Grain coarsening (grains grow
  too large), which REDUCES mechanical properties. The system over-
  processes and loses necessary structure.

  Proxy variable: Annealing time, or more precisely, the time-temperature
  integral (Hollomon-Jaffe parameter or equivalent).

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

ΔC (CONTRADICTION LOAD) — Internal stress / dislocation density

  When a metal is deformed (hammered, rolled, drawn, bent), atoms are
  forced out of their equilibrium positions. Crystal defects multiply.
  Internal stress accumulates. This is the ΔC of the system.

  C1 (Acute): Surface-level deformation. Minor cold work. Elastic
  deformation that would recover on its own if load removed.

  C2 (Structural): Significant plastic deformation. Dislocation density
  increased by orders of magnitude. Work hardening has occurred. The
  metal's microstructure is fundamentally altered. Cannot reverse
  without energy input (annealing).

  C3 (Existential): Fatigue damage. Micro-cracks have nucleated.
  Damage is approaching the point of catastrophic failure. The
  system's structural integrity is compromised at the deepest level.

  Proxy variable: Dislocation density (dislocations/m²), internal
  stress (MPa), micro-crack density.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

E* (EMERGENCE) — Recrystallised microstructure

  After successful annealing, the metal has a NEW grain structure. It is
  not restored to its original pre-deformation state — it is a new
  configuration that has processed the accumulated contradiction (ΔC)
  and reached a new low-energy equilibrium.

  Key prediction: E* is NOVEL, not restorative. The recrystallised
  grain structure is different from both the deformed state AND the
  original pre-deformation state. New grain sizes, new grain
  orientations, potentially new phase distributions.

  Stability assessment: Mechanical properties (hardness, ductility,
  tensile strength) stabilise at new values. Internal stress reduced
  to near-zero. Grain structure stable at operating temperature.

  Proxy variable: Grain size distribution, hardness values, ductility
  measurements, residual stress measurements.
