𝔉 (FORGIVENESS) — Ductile deformation capacity / creep

  The Earth's crust and upper mantle exist along a continuum from
  BRITTLE (cold, shallow, rigid) to DUCTILE (hot, deep, flowing).

  𝔉 in plate tectonics = the material's capacity to accommodate
  tectonic stress through continuous, non-catastrophic deformation.
  This is mediated by:
  - Temperature: hotter rock flows more readily (ductile)
  - Pressure and depth: deeper rock under greater confining pressure
    can deform plastically rather than fracture
  - Mineralogy: some minerals (e.g., olivine, quartz at depth) deform
    ductilely; others (feldspar at shallow depth) fracture
  - Fluids: presence of water/fluids at faults can either enable
    creep (lubricating) or increase pore pressure (reducing effective
    stress)

  Higher 𝔉 = more ductile behaviour = stress is accommodated through
  continuous flow, creep, or slow slip without sudden fracture.
  Lower 𝔉 = more brittle = stress accumulates until sudden rupture.

  𝔉 = 0 prediction: A perfectly rigid, zero-ductility material cannot
  accommodate ANY tectonic stress through flow. ALL stress accumulates
  until catastrophic fracture. This should produce the most violent,
  destructive earthquakes — maximum stored energy released instantly.

  𝔉 = ∞ prediction: A perfectly ductile material (asthenosphere /
  deep mantle) flows continuously and never fractures. Stress is
  accommodated in real time — no earthquakes, but also no mountain
  building, no sharp structural features, no emergence of rigid
  geological structures.

  Proxy variable: Brittle-ductile transition depth, temperature
  gradient, creep rate, fault coupling coefficient.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

T (TIME) — Duration of stress accumulation and release cycle

  Tectonic stress does not build and release instantaneously. The
  SEISMIC CYCLE involves:
  - Inter-seismic period: stress accumulates on a locked fault
  - Co-seismic period: sudden rupture and stress release (earthquake)
  - Post-seismic period: continued adjustment (aftershocks, creep,
    viscoelastic relaxation)

  T_min = insufficient processing time. If the seismic cycle is
  interrupted or if stress is released before full elastic strain
  accumulates (e.g., through induced seismicity from fluid injection
  at small magnitude), the large-scale stress field may not be
  fully reorganised. Premature release = incomplete processing.

  T_viable = the natural seismic cycle where stress accumulates to
  a level that, when released, produces meaningful reorganisation —
  slip on faults, topographic change, strain redistribution. The
  system processes the contradiction and reaches a new (temporary)
  equilibrium.

  T_max = excessively prolonged stress accumulation. When a fault is
  LOCKED for far longer than its natural recurrence interval (fully
  coupled, no creep, no small releases), the eventual rupture
  produces a MEGA-EARTHQUAKE — far more destructive than the regular
  cycle would produce. Cascading failure. Tsunami generation.

  Proxy variable: Recurrence interval, elapsed time since last
  major rupture, post-seismic relaxation time, aftershock duration.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

ΔC (CONTRADICTION LOAD) — Tectonic driving force / stress

  The "contradiction" is the incompatible motion of adjacent tectonic
  plates. Where plates converge, diverge, or slide past each other,
  STRESS accumulates in the rock.

  C1 (Minor): Low-stress plate interiors. Intraplate regions far from
  boundaries. Stress is minimal, deformation slow, seismicity rare.

  C2 (Structural): Active plate boundaries with moderate convergence
  rates. Typical subduction zones, transform faults, spreading ridges.
  Regular earthquake cycles, mountain building, volcanic activity.
  The system is under sustained but manageable load.

  C3 (Existential): Collision zones with extreme convergence rates,
  deeply coupled locked faults (megathrust zones), or catastrophic
  events (mega-earthquakes M9+). The ΔC exceeds local processing
  capacity, causing widespread destruction, tsunami, and regional
  reorganisation.

  SUPPRESSED ΔC: Locked fault segments. When a section of a fault
  is fully coupled (no creep, no slip, no small earthquakes), stress
  accumulates silently for decades or centuries. The locked segment
  appears quiet — low seismicity, no surface deformation — but
  elastic strain energy is building. This is the seismic equivalent
  of fire suppression (Test #2), metal fatigue (Test #3), and HIV
  latency (Test #4): invisible ΔC accumulation leading to eventual
  catastrophic release.

  Proxy variable: Plate velocity, convergence rate, fault coupling
  coefficient, seismic moment deficit, Coulomb stress change.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

E* (EMERGENCE) — Novel geological structures

  Tectonic activity produces PERMANENT NEW STRUCTURES that did not
  exist before the contradiction:

  - Mountain ranges (Himalayas from India-Eurasia collision)
  - Ocean trenches (from subduction)
  - Rift valleys and new ocean basins (from divergence)
  - Metamorphic rock (existing rock transformed by tectonic pressure
    and heat into new mineral assemblages)
  - New continental configurations (plate movements over geological
    time produce novel continent positions)
  - Fault scarps, displaced terrain, reorganised drainage systems

  Key prediction: E* is NOVEL, not restorative. After a major
  tectonic event (earthquake, orogeny, rifting), the landscape does
  not return to its pre-event state. Mountain ranges are NEW features.
  Fault scarps are permanent offsets. Metamorphic rock is a new
  mineral state, not the original rock. The system goes THROUGH the
  contradiction and emerges with new structure.

  Proxy variable: Topographic change, metamorphic grade, new fault
  geometry, crustal thickness change, seismic moment release.
